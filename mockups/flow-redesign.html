Menu-bar UX teardown · Tickle

The escalating cue,
made visible.

Tickle paces an interview by playing a growing burst of ticks — one at the first mark, two at the second, up to nine — as the window runs down. Today that idea is invisible until a blocking alert scolds you. This is a redesign of the whole flow around it.

02

One surface. Always valid. Self-explaining.

The separate panel collapses into an inline composer inside the menu-bar popover — anchored to the icon, with a live tick-mark rail and controls that cannot express an invalid timer. Drag the sliders below; the rail, the derived window, and the single Start button all respond in real time.

The centerpiece

The composer lives where you clicked.

No screen-centered window, no context switch. The popover drops from the icon and holds everything: your running timers up top, a new-timer composer below.

The escalating burst that defines Tickle is finally shown — a rail of marks whose height climbs 1 → N as the window closes, with a finish flag for the double chime.

  • 01
    Valid by constructionThe window slider’s ceiling is 9 × interval. You physically cannot author a 10-mark timer, so the blocking alert disappears entirely.
  • 02
    The model is the previewEvery mark you’ll hear is on screen, sized by its tick count. “N ticks at the Nth mark, max 9” stops being lore.
  • 03
    One adaptive commit“Now / Later” is a toggle, not two buttons. The single Start button rewrites itself — “Start now” or “Start at 11:48 PM.”
  • 04
    Edit after launchRunning timers show the same rail and an inline Adjust — pacing is legible and tunable, not frozen in a submenu.

Before · the alert-driven panel
After · the inline composer
A separate window opens centered on screen, disconnected from the icon you clicked.
A popover is anchored to the menu-bar icon with a caret — one continuous gesture.
Invalid configs (>9 marks, elapsed start) are allowed, then rejected by a modal after Start.
Controls are clamped to the valid range; the alert is deleted because it can never trigger.
Two competing buttons — “Start Now” and “Schedule Start” — split the commit.
A Now/Later toggle + one adaptive button that relabels itself to the actual outcome.
The interval/window/marks relationship is opaque until it errors in red text.
A live tick-mark rail renders every cue as you compose — the rule is on screen.
A launched timer is a dense text row; you can only Stop or Reset it.
Running timers carry the same rail and an inline Adjust — legible and editable.
