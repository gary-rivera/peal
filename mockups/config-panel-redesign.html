Design Review · macOS AppKit

Tickle's New Timer panel,
rebuilt around what it does.

The current panel asks you to configure a metronome you can't see. Interval and window are two disconnected sliders; whether they produce a valid pattern only surfaces as a line of red text. The redesign makes the escalating tick pattern visible and turns setup into direct manipulation of that pattern.

The one big idea

A live tick-pattern timeline

Tickle plays a growing burst at each interval mark — 1 tick at the 1st mark, 2 at the 2nd, up to 9 — then a double chime when the window ends. That escalating rhythm is the whole product, yet the old panel never shows it. The centerpiece is a timeline that lays every mark across the window in real time: you literally see the beats stack up, watch the 9-mark ceiling as a physical limit, and feel interval-vs-window as one relationship instead of two lonely sliders and an error string.

Current panel — top 5 issues

  1. Invisible mental model. The escalating "N ticks at the Nth mark" pattern — the app's core behavior — is never shown, so users configure blind.
  2. Meaningless slider precision. 60 and 120 tick marks jammed onto ~180px tracks give a dense, un-hittable comb where single-unit accuracy is impossible.
  3. Error as afterthought. The invalid state ("1 min · 10 ticks") is just red text wedged into a value column, easy to miss and offering no in-place remedy.
  4. Split, ambiguous start actions. "Start Now" and "Schedule Start" sit in different grid columns with equal weight, hiding which is primary and how they relate.
  5. Broken alignment & redundant value/unit. Right-ragged labels, unit popups landing at different x-positions per row, and the value echoed as both text and a popup fracture the grid.
